PermitKey: A harmonized national work-type classification of 150 million U.S. building permit records

Candy Martinez∗1, Sean Hardesty Lewis†1, Yichun Fan‡2, and Siqi Zheng§1
1Sustainable Urbanization Lab and Center for Real Estate, Massachusetts Institute of Technology, Cambridge, MA, USA
2Nicholas School of the Environment, Duke University, Durham, NC, USA
Abstract Method Code Data

PermitKey turns fragmented public permit systems into a comparable research frame.

Abstract

Building permits are among the most complete records of physical change to the U.S. built environment, but local labels differ across jurisdictions and cannot be compared directly. PermitKey harmonizes 150,044,555 retained source records from 85 contributions into a shared 25-category work-type vocabulary while preserving provenance, dates, locations, and source missingness.

The resulting derived aggregates are designed for research on construction, housing supply, energy upgrades, climate policy, and urban change. The extended abstract describes the data model, weak-supervision procedure, validation plan, and release design.

Method

Collect and preserve

PermitKey begins with public records from local permitting portals. Each source contribution is retained as a source-record occurrence with its native labels, description text, dates, coordinates, portal provenance, and stable identifiers. The pipeline records coverage and missingness instead of silently treating unavailable fields as negative evidence.

Harmonize work types

Jurisdiction-supplied labels provide weak supervision: they are abundant but administratively defined and mutually incomparable. We normalize the source fields, train independent text classifiers against an analyst-specified taxonomy, and allow multiple work types on one record because a permit can authorize several jobs at once. The current reference taxonomy contains 25 documented categories spanning construction, alteration, demolition, building systems, exterior work, sitework, and energy technologies.

Validate and aggregate

Model outputs are checked against source labels and blind human review, with class-specific performance and uncertainty reported separately. Only after eligibility, geography, source-rights, and disclosure checks do we form derived products at permitting-jurisdiction × year × work type and, for reliably geocoded records, 0.1° grid × year × work type. These aggregates are intended to support reproducible comparisons without publishing raw permit-level records.

Data release

Preparation in progress

The copied collection and candidate pipeline have passed integrity smokes. Geography mapping, source-rights review, disclosure controls, and candidate validation remain before public downloads are enabled.

Open release status

The release will contain derived aggregates rather than raw permit-level records. Jurisdiction and grid products are distinct tables. Work-type counts can overlap because one source record may carry multiple labels.
